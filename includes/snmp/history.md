The Simple Network Management Protocol (SNMP) emerged in the late 1980s as a response to proprietary solutions to network management which did not combine data from multiple vendors.
SNMP evolved out of an effort by the IETF to modify the earlier Simple Gateway Monitoring Protocol (SGMP) 

- SNMPv1, published in 1990, emphasized simplicity and introduced a lightweight manager-agent model as well as the MIB.
- SNMPv2 fixed functional gaps but disputes over its security model delayed its release.
- SNMPV2c (community-based SNMPv2) preserved the simple community strings of v1 while adding bulk retrieval operations and more granular error handling.
- SNMPv3, ratified in 2002, addressed the security deficiencies of v1 and v2c.
